S1.D1b

The headline face — why Lora is the convention here

The challenge on the table: chores and calendar render user-typed titles in DM Sans, so isn't a Lora meal title a divergence? Those are row-scale titles — 16px text-card-title, and the meal name in a week-plan row (S2) stays exactly that. At display scale the type system has no sans rung: every text token above 20px is Lora (page-title 26 · display-lg 29 · display-xl 36), and DM Sans's only large token is text-metric — numerals only, by the XP/coin rule. The across-room precedent, the feed banner, is Lora prose. Both faces rendered honestly:

ALora displayRecommended

The scale's own display rung, and the register that says family menu, not app chrome — the same voice as page titles and the fridge-wall banner.

Two flavours of empty: planned but nobody's on it (title exists, owner null) and nothing planned at all. Both keep the menu-card anatomy — the headline stays the headline, the sides line states the gap, the rail carries the claim. The gate is whether it stays polite all day or gets louder as dinner approaches.

Muted statement + ghost claim button, identical at 9am and 6pm. Honest and calm — but at 4pm on a Thursday it whispers the one fact the house most needs shouted.

AInitial-in-tint + glyphRecommended

Rendered above: 16px identity-tint disc carrying the initial, muted glyph beside it. Both channels ADR 044 demands — hue and letter — survive 16px; a photo at that size is mud from across the kitchen.

BAvatar photo + glyph

The real face where one exists, initial fallback. Warmer up close, but a 16px photo carries neither hue nor letter at fridge distance — it reads as “someone”. Defensible only if marks also render larger somewhere, which they don't.

CGlyph in tinted squareFails ADR 044

One element — utensils inside an identity-tint square. Tidiest, and disqualified: the mark becomes hue-only, exactly the surface the second-channel rule exists for. Listed so it stays rejected on the record.

S3.D2

Agenda header label

The mark's one line of words, in the mobile agenda's day header. The what stays on meals' surfaces — this is attribution only.

A“Dinner · Zach”

Fact-first, scans in a dense agenda, matches how school marks will read (“No school”). Neutral to a fault.

B“Zach's got dinner”Recommended

The module's own voice — the queue, the nudge and the widget all say “who's got dinner”, and the label keeping that verb keeps the product's one sentence intact. Two words longer; the agenda header has the room.

S3.D3

Unclaimed on the calendar

A planned-but-unclaimed dinner: does the calendar say so?

ANo markRecommended

The calendar is a record, not a nag — an ownerless mark has no who, and the who is the entire payload (ADR 050). The widget and the 4pm nudge already own the pressure. Thursday above renders this: bare.

BHollow mark

A dashed ring + glyph, mirroring the week-strip's hollow chip. Consistent with S1.D3-A — but it puts a to-do on a surface whose grammar is facts, and it's the first calendar element that means “somebody act”.

S4.D1

A · Sticky bar + staples chips Recommended
Always-visible input, staples one tap beneath — the “we're out of milk” path is glance → tap → done.

B · FAB + sheet
A floating button opening a compose sheet. Two taps before typing starts; the sheet hides the staples row that does half the adds. Rejected for capture speed, not rendered.

S4.D2

A · Newest first, flat Recommended
Capture order, most recent on top — confirmation your add landed, and dedupe-by-eye (“milk's already on there”).

B · Aisle groups in build too
One mental model across both modes, but it scatters each new add into a bucket mid-list, so the fridge-side glance loses “what just went on”. Grouping is shop's job.

S4.D3

A · Optional chip moment at add Recommended
After an add, a transient aisle chip row (rendered above). One tap files it; ignoring it files under Other. The person at the fridge knows the aisle; the person in the store shouldn't have to.

B · Default Other, sort later
Purest three-second add, but it exports the sorting to the aisle — the exact place the spec says never to do work.

S5.D1

A · Whole row checks Recommended
Anywhere on the row toggles; tap again untoggles. The 64px row is the target — one thumb, cart in the other hand. Nothing on the row is destructive, so the fat target is safe.

B · Ring only
Only the 28px ring toggles. Precision nobody asked for, at exactly the motor moment the spec says to design against.

S5.D2

A · Fixed walk order Recommended
Produce → Bakery → Meat → Dairy → Pantry → Frozen → Other: the perimeter-then-middle shape of nearly every US grocery store, shipped as a constant.

B · Per-family reorder
Drag-to-reorder aisle sections in settings. Real feature, real story — deferred until the fixed order actually annoys someone; noted for the M5/7 kickoff as a non-goal.

S5.D3

A · Hue dot on the checked row Recommended
The partner's 16px initial disc appears on rows they checked (rendered above) — identity earning its place on a two-person surface, with the name in the row's accessible text.

B · Banner presence
“Sam is also shopping” pinned at top. Says they exist, not what they've done — the fact you need mid-aisle is per-item. Could join A later; not instead of it.

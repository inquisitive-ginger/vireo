What the critique changed, what it didn't

Accepted — it was right

  • Localised escalation over the full --warning wash. Four widgets washing amber at 5pm is a dashboard crying wolf; the habituation cost lands on the alert that matters. The badge moves the same distance without taking the field.
  • Date out of the widget header — the screen states it already.
  • Week strip: today marked, count in words ("2 days need a cook") — the label does the tallying a person shouldn't.
  • Groceries as ListRow — hairline rows, rings, aisle heads. Forty bordered cards was real chrome debt; the density problem was mine.
  • Filing dictionary — auto-classify on add, ask only when unknown. Better than my chip-row-every-add: zero friction for "milk", one tap for "quinoa".
  • Cleared-aisle collapse ("Produce · all 4 in the cart") — earned space, satisfying, reversible.
  • The wall is its own surface — columns so the whole list fits, staples beside the add field, "Sam is at the store" presence. Designing the surface instead of porting the phone.
  • The narrowed hue rule — hue where the person is the answer to the screen's question. Grocery spines were decoration with a rationale; meals spines are the point. Goes to an ADR amendment, below.
  • Verb-kind consistency — v2's S2 row wearing a Cooked chip after S1 retired it was an inconsistency, plainly.

Pushed back — intentional

  • The wall is 1060px, not 1180. The house's wall is the fridge iPad — design-system.md §Grid: four columns fill ~1060px after the 220px sidebar at 1280. There is no 1920 panel in this deployment. Columns survive; there are three of them. (Also moots half the column-rebalance worry: three columns re-flow less than four.)
  • The week plan stays on ItemCard — by the critique's own proposed rule: "cards when items are independent objects with several actions; rows when they're a set you scan and act on one way." A day row has edit, claim, and needs-push — three actions on an independent object. That's the card contract, and it's what chores and calendar do. ListRow is for the grocery list, which is exactly the scan-and-act-one-way set the rule describes.
  • "Last Thursday you did tacos" is gated, not shipped. Lovely line, but spec.md defers meal history deliberately, and this is history's thin edge. The empty state ships with neutral copy; the history prompt is a marked fast-follow if M5/2 finds the one-row query free.

On approval

What close-out writes

Two ADR-shaped decisions surfaced by this session — they get real ADRs, not silent adoption:
1. ListRow joins ItemCard with the written rule: cards for independent objects with several actions; rows for a set you scan and act on one way. First consumers: grocery list (rows), week plan (cards — the rule applied, not an exception).
2. The hue rule narrows (amends ADR 043/044 application): identity hue appears where the person is the answer to the surface's question — not on every multi-person surface. Groceries loses all identity chrome; meals keeps spines.
Spec/schema ripples: planned_meals.sides text nullable · grocery category is the dictionary-assigned aisle — ~200 keywords shipping, plus a per-family override learned from corrections (re-filing an item teaches it; one small table) · unknown → Other + ask on the row · shop-session presence ("Sam is at the store") noted for M5/7, derived from check activity, no new tables. Spec-language amendment, stated rather than slid past: "checked items dim in place, never vanish" gains a reconciliation — a fully cleared aisle may collapse to a stated row ("Bakery · all 2 in the cart", tap to reopen). The rule exists to answer "did I already get that?", and the stated row answers it better than dimmed lines; individual checked items still never vanish. Design-system entries: the localised-escalation pattern (badge + solid action, never a full wash — dashboards must stay diagnosable) and the menu-card widget anatomy.

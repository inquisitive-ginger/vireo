Vireo · M5.5 Shopping · live design critique 4 Sep 2026

The list you cannot take anything off

Capture is fast and the walk order works. The gap is everywhere after the add: a shopper on a phone has exactly one verb — got / not got — and every other thing a person needs to do to a row is either stranded on the tablet, gated to a parent, or absent from the product entirely. Below: what I saw running the real app, then the direction, then the work broken out for the polish session.

The direction

The row keeps one tap. Its two edges carry everything else — right for the full set, left for the one verb you actually reach for.

Right now the row is a switch with a second button bolted to it, and the verbs that didn't fit — remove, rename, take back — went somewhere else or nowhere. That's the thing to fix, and it doesn't need a mode, an index, or a drill-in. Three moves:

Two edges, one row

Drag left (revealing the right edge) for the full permitted set: Remove · Move to aisle · Rename · Note. Drag right (revealing the left edge) for remove alone, the verb worth a shortcut. Long-press and Shift+F10 both reach the full set. The permanent trailing icon comes off every row.

The add gets out of the way

Capture's job is to catch the word and be ready for the next one — so it stops narrating aisles it can't let you fix, and says only the one thing you can act on in that second: Already on Grocery. Take it back? Filing is real work, but it's list-view work, done once before you walk in.

Give the walk an ending

The check is the most-repeated act in the product and it's the least rewarding one: a faint ring flips, silently. It should feel like putting something in a cart — and clearing the last aisle should say so, without taking away the field or the aisles you might still open.

What's missing

7 findings

The functional gaps, in the order they'd bite a real family.

SH-01

Nothing can be removed from the list — by anyone

Blocker

Not on the phone, not on the tablet, not as a parent. There is no delete on a row at any tier. The server has had the endpoint since M5.5/2 and no screen has ever called it.

Seen: I added milk and paper towels, then went looking for a way to take either off the list itself. As Family (T1) and as Zoë (T3) there is no control on a row but the check ring. In source, DELETE /shopping/items/:id exists, is gated to tier 4, and has zero client callers — so even you can't press it.

SH-02

The one undo that exists is stranded on the tablet

Blocker

ADR 073 gives every tier fifteen minutes to take back their own add. That affordance renders only on the Just added chips, and those only exist on the wide capture layout. A phone is always in list mode — so the surface most adds come from is the one surface that can never undo one.

Seen: to undo my two test adds I had to leave the phone layout, go to the tablet view, and click the × on a chip. The x never appears at 375px because the strip itself doesn't.

SH-03

Duplicates are accepted silently, then separated

Blocker

Adding something already on the list makes a second row, filed by add-time, so the twin doesn't even land next to its original. This is the exact failure mode of a shared list — two people add milk, the shopper buys two.

Seen: with milk already in Dairy I typed milk again. No warning, count went 16 → 17, and Dairy rendered milk · greek yogurt · butter · milk — the duplicate three rows below the original, one of them struck through after I checked the first. Separately, paper towels is now on both Grocery and Costco and neither screen mentions the other.

SH-04

Non-food has no aisle, so "Other" is a second list

Should fix

There are seven aisles and all seven are food: Produce, Bakery, Meat, Dairy, Pantry, Frozen, Other. Every paper good, cleaner, toiletry, battery and bin bag in a normal grocery run lands in Other — which sits at the end of a walk order it was never part of. The premise of the surface is "a list already in walk order"; for a quarter of a real list it isn't.

Seen: typing paper towels printed files under Other. In the seeded list, Other already held 3 of 17 items.

SH-05

Only a parent can fix a misfile — and parents aren't the ones making them

Should fix

The aisle correction is file, tier 4. Kids and the kitchen wall can put things in Other all week and can't move one. The correction teaches a family-wide override, which is a fair reason to price it — but the person standing in front of the wrong aisle is the one who can't act.

Seen: as Zoë (T3) the control is absent from every row, not disabled — no hint the aisle is even changeable.

SH-06

Nothing can be edited — a typo is permanent

Should fix

No rename, no quantity, no note from any screen. Combined with SH-01, a mistyped row is immortal.

Seen: the seeded list carries mikk twice. Nobody in the family can fix or remove either one.

SH-07

The shop has no ending

Polish

There's a state for an empty list and a state for a cleared aisle, but none for a finished trip. Check the last item and you're left on a screen of collapsed grey summary rows and a full progress bar — the shape of an empty page, not the feeling of a finished one.

Seen: cleared Produce collapsed correctly to Produce · 5 in the cart. There is no all-clear branch in the view source — only the zero-items empty state.

The row, and its clutter

2 findings
SH-08

A permanent control for a once-a-month act

Should fix

Your instinct is right, and the design system agrees with you: ADR 063 chose ListRow precisely because these are "a set you scan and act on one way." A second per-row button contradicts the reason the primitive was picked. It also inverts priority — the correction glyph is more visible than the check ring you press thirty times a trip.

In source: the trailing button is h-11 w-11 — 44px, under the 48px --spacing-touch floor — wrapping a 14px FolderInput glyph whose meaning isn't guessable. It renders on every row, for tier 4, always.

SH-09

Two edges, and the first gesture the app has ever had

Should fix

Settled direction: right edge opens the full permitted set, left edge is a shortcut to remove. That's a good split — it puts the verb you reach for ninety percent of the time one motion away without hardwiring a gesture to the only destructive act. Two things it costs, both worth paying: there is no touch-gesture handling anywhere in the plugin today, so this is new interaction vocabulary for Vireo and belongs in @family-os/ui beside ListRow; and the quick remove needs an undo rather than a confirm, because a confirm dialog on the fast path defeats the reason it's the fast path.

Constraint: ADR 068 gives the row 48px live and 8px dead; a horizontal gesture must not eat the vertical tap that is still the row's primary action. And: removal is a hard delete on both paths (ADR 073 §4) — so "undo" means re-creating the row with a new id and a reset retract clock. See E1 for the two ways to spend that.

Three counting schemes on one screen, two of them contradicting

Blocker

The queue already has the "colliding 14s" as a coincidence problem. It's worse than that: the pill counts what's left, the meter counts what's done out of the total, and the title counts what's left across all lists. On Costco that renders as a flat contradiction.

SH-11

The sticky status line drops the list name

Should fix

SH-12

Polish

The meter is correctly hidden at zero — but its arrival pushes every row down by its own height, once, at the exact moment your finger is moving to the next row. Reserve the space, or grow the meter in from zero height.

Seen: ~58px downward shift of the whole list on the first check of a trip.

Capture, and joy

2 findings
SH-13

Capture is narrating a fact nobody can act on

Should fix

The filing echo states an aisle before the commit so a misfile is caught by the person who caused it — except the person who caused it usually can't fix it (SH-05), and by your own account the aisle isn't a decision anyone makes at capture time. It's decided once, in the list, before a trip. So the echo spends the one line under the field on information that arrives too early to be useful and too gated to be actionable. Reclaim that line for the collision notice, which is the thing you can act on in that second.

Worth knowing: this reverses design.md §Anatomy item 3 and touches ADR 070's amendment, which explicitly preserves the filing echo. It's your call to make, but it's a doc amendment, not a polish tweak — the ADR should be updated in the same PR. Also: the chips are a hand-rolled <span>, not the Chip primitive the pills beside them use, so their focus and hover states don't match.

SH-14

The wall is mostly empty, and the check is unrewarded

Polish

Two joy gaps at opposite ends. On the tablet capture screen, roughly half the height below the chips is blank — a room-facing surface with nothing to look at between adds. And the single most repeated gesture in the product, the check, is a silent ring flip with no weight to it.

Measured: tablet capture — content ends ~440px, footer at ~944px of 1024. Phone list — first row at 428px of 812, four of twelve rows visible on arrival.

Deliberately not reported

Already yours Voice and the hidden nav item, per your brief. Already in the polish queue and not re-litigated here: the check ring's 1.29:1 contrast (it is the root of SH-08's priority inversion, so the two fixes meet), the 2×2 Home tile's dashboard cost, the add-error strip erasing a previous failure, and section-label's missing block-spacing rule. Settled and untouched: no modes, no list index, no per-row attribution (ADR 064), nothing autofocuses (ADR 070).

How this was run

Caveat worth knowing I don't enter authentication PINs, so the tier-4 pass didn't happen live — I audited as Family (tier 1) and Zoë (tier 3), and everything tier-4-only (the aisle-correction button's size, glyph and placement; the unreachable delete endpoint) is read from source rather than seen on screen. One edge, every verb

SH-09 · unblocks 01 / 06 / 08

The keystone, now with two edges. Drag left and the right edge offers the full set, filtered by what this person may do to this row. Drag right and the left edge offers remove alone — the verb that earns a shortcut. The row itself never grows a control, which is how the trailing button in E2 comes off. Vertical tap stays the check, untouched.

1Both edges are the same permission check. If remove isn't permitted on this row, the left edge doesn't move — it never reveals a button that will fail.
2Left edge reveals, it doesn't fire. One drag plus one tap, no dialog. A full-swipe-to-delete variant is faster and is the other option — see the spec for what it costs.
3Verbs run least to most destructive, remove last and in --error. The keys on the right are for you, not shipping.
4One verb still gets a sheet on the right edge — consistency beats the saved tap, and it's one code path. The shortcut is the left edge's whole job.
Spec Left edge (drag right): reveal 112px, threshold 56px, snap back below it; the revealed control is Remove or Take it back depending on which permission granted it, and fires on tap. Right edge (drag left): reveal 96px, threshold 96px or 40% of row width, whichever is smaller; opens the sheet on release. Only one row is open at a time; a new drag or a scroll closes the last. Axis lock on the first 8px — horizontal claims the gesture, vertical hands it to scroll. The activation overlay (inset-y-[3.5px], ADR 068) keeps working while the row is offset, and a drag never fires the check. Keyboard: Shift+F10 / context-menu key opens the sheet, Escape closes and restores focus. Pointer: long-press 500ms, right-click on desktop. There is no keyboard shortcut for the quick path — it's a shortcut, not a second API. Zero permitted verbs → no gesture is bound on either edge. All motion behind prefers-reduced-motion.

The undo question, which needs your answer before this ships. Removal is a hard delete on both paths (ADR 073 §4 — no retracted status, no soft delete), so there is nothing to restore. Two ways to spend that: (a) reveal-then-tap, drawn above — one extra tap, no undo needed, no data question; (b) full-swipe fires immediately plus Removed · Undo in the report slot for ~5s, where Undo re-creates the row with a new id and a reset added_at. (b) is faster and is what Mail-style gestures train people to expect; it also means an undo silently changes the row's identity, which matters to the retract clock and to anything holding the old id. Recommend (a) for this session and (b) as a follow-up once quantity lands and rows have a merge story.

Capture stops talking about aisles

SH-13 · reverses design.md §Anatomy 3

Your call, and I think it's the right one: filing is a list-view job done once before a trip, not a fact worth a line under the field every time someone types a word. The echo's original justification was that a misfile gets caught by whoever caused it — but the people doing most of the capturing can't correct an aisle at all (SH-05), so the line has been reporting a problem to an audience that can't fix it. The chip goes back to being a name and a way to undo it.

1The echo keeps its reserved height either way — it's what stops the first keystroke shoving the layout, and it's where E4's collision line lands.
2On the right every chip carries an ×, because on the wall every one of them is yours (ADR 072 §3). The ragged strip I flagged is really a phone-only artifact — worth checking it doesn't read as arbitrary there.
3Dropping the echo removes the only place the aisle vocabulary is visible before a trip. That makes SH-04 harder to notice and more important to fix — nobody will see Other filling up until they're standing in the shop.
4Chips stay hand-rolled <span>s today; moving them onto Chip is the small, unrelated win in this panel.
Spec Remove the aisle clause from the echo in all three surfaces — ShoppingPageView.tsx:228, ShoppingCaptureHero.tsx:103, CaptureWidgetView.tsx:49 — and the draftAisle plumbing behind them (onDraftChange, use-ambient-capture.ts). Keep the slot and its min-h-10 reserve. Amend design.md §Anatomy item 3 and ADR 070's 2026-09-04 amendment in the same PR, since both name the filing echo as preserved — leaving the docs describing a line that no longer exists is how the next session re-adds it. Chips rebuild on Chip (ShoppingCaptureHero.tsx:122–141), keep aria-label="Take back {name}", and take eligibility from the server's canRetract, never from position in the strip (ADR 073 §2). No hue, no avatar, no adder name on any chip (ADR 072 §3, ADR 064).

Open, and it changes what "filing" means: "reorganize the aisles in the list view" could be moving items into the right aisle, or reordering the aisles themselves to match a store's walk. The second is currently recorded as a named non-goal (aisle-dictionary.ts: "Per-family reorder is a named non-goal") and would make walk order a property of the list rather than of the app — a bigger change than it sounds, and a product story. Worth answering before SH-05 gets designed.

The collision is caught where it happens

SH-03

Cut to your wording. The notice is the fact; the verb is the only thing to do about it. Nothing about where it filed, nothing about it having been added anyway — the row is visibly there, so saying so is a sentence the reader has to parse and can't use.

1Both screens still show two milks. Deliberate — the notice stops the silence, it does not solve the duplicate.
2Take it back removes the add that just happened, never the row that was already there. Worth stating plainly for the planner, since on a shared list those are two different people's rows.
3Match key is normalizeItemName, which already exists — so Milk, and milk collide, and mikk correctly does not.
4Unchecked rows only. Re-adding something already in the cart is a real second-trip item, not a mistake.
Spec Copy, verbatim: Already on Grocery. as the notice, Take it back? as the action, list name interpolated. Lives in the existing min-h-10 report slot; must not resize it or steal focus, and never blocks — ADR 070's field clears and stays ready. Resolve against queue item 4 (a failed add erased by the next success) before building; both write to this one slot and the ordering rule has to be decided once. The action targets the just-created item's id and is the same retract call as the chip ×; it is absent if that add is not retract-eligible. Server returns the collision alongside the created item so the client needs no second read.

On quantity — agreed, punt it, with one decision made now. A dedicated session is right; it touches the schema, the meals push, and the check semantics (is milk ×2 one check or two?). But decide now whether a duplicate add will eventually merge into the existing row or stay a second row carrying a count, because that determines whether this notice is a stopgap that gets deleted or the permanent behaviour for the "no, genuinely two separate things" case. If it's merge, this notice is temporary and shouldn't grow features. If it's a count, this notice is where the count gets offered later, and the slot should be built with room for a second action.

The walk gets an ending

Clear the last item today and the screen you're left holding is a stack of grey summary rows and a full bar — structurally identical to an empty page. This is the cheapest joy in the product and the only moment in a shop that is unambiguously good news.

SH-01

Row removal, permission-filtered

Blocker
Evidence
No delete affordance on any row at any tier, either route, both viewports. DELETE /shopping/items/:id (apps/api/src/plugins/shopping.ts:436, gated can('manage','shopping.Item')) has zero client callers.
Touches
plugins/shopping/src/pages/ShoppingPageView.tsx (Row ~417–455, ShoppingPageViewProps 24–74), ShoppingPage.tsx (new mutation beside retractItem ~287)
Constraints
ADR 073 §5 — a removal emits shopping.item.removed with reason: 'managed' | 'retracted'; the managed path currently emits and the client must invalidate on it. ADR 018 — gate on ability.can, never a tier number.
Depends on
SH-09 (the affordance it lands in)
  • A row exposes remove when can('manage','shopping.Item') OR the item's canRetract is true; both paths hit their own endpoint.
  • The two verbs read differently to the user: taking back your own recent add vs. removing something from the list.
  • A 403 at the retract boundary renders its message rather than being swallowed (ADR 073 §2) — reuse the existing "too old to take back" branch at ShoppingPage.tsx:305–309.
  • Removal is reachable at 375px, which is the whole point.

Blocker

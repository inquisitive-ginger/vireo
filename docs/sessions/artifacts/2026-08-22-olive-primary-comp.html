The primary moves to olive

Terracotta hands --primary and --ring to an olive green — the vireo's own plumage. Everything else holds: the eight identity hues, teal accent, warm clay neutrals, coin brass, status colors. Baseline first, then three candidates around moss (117°). Judge the moss member's spine against the buttons in every frame — that adjacency is the decision.

Round 2 · after Deep Olive shipped to the branch

The ground under the olive

The clay neutrals (hue 45–80°) were cut for terracotta — an analogous primary whose washes read as warmer cream. Olive sits 50° away, so a 10% olive wash over yellow-cream lands in the muddy in-between: the active nav pill reads stained, not chosen. Three ways out, judged on the surface that exposed it — nav rail with an active pill and a hover, muted text, a grouped row, a primary button.

Recommendation

I'd ship B — Deep Olive. It's the truest "olive" of the three, it beats terracotta's text contrast in both modes (5.8 and 3.5 vs today's 4.8 and 3.3), and by sitting a step deeper and quieter than --identity-moss it keeps a moss member's spine clearly distinct from every button — the harmonized-with-primary role still transfers from terracotta to moss, but without the exact coincidence.

A is the principled straight port (terracotta's exact L/C on moss's hue) and the boldest of the three; if Vireo should feel vivid rather than earthy, it's the pick — its dark-mode white-on-primary sits at 3.0, the same tolerance terracotta already accepts. C is the closest to literal olive-drab but shares a family with coin brass (90° vs 108°) — visible in the Chores card where the F$ chip sits over a green button.
